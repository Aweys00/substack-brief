# Substack brief: 15 November 2026

First brief, covering the 12 posts published 9 to 14 November. Six are full text and six are paywalled previews (marked below).

## The short version

Nvidia lost its CEO on Thursday, and Tae Kim argues the stock at about $160 is cheap even in a bad scenario; earnings are on Tuesday 17 November and the Financial Times reports Jensen Huang is exploring a comeback, so the situation is still moving. Separately, the AI infrastructure selloff that followed the Texas election is being read by Vik as a reason to favour anything that gets more compute out of an existing data center, in particular 800 V power delivery. CoreWeave's results eased the financing worry that had been driving the selloff. Anthropic cut frontier model prices 20%, which Jamin Ball reads as efficiency gains passed through, and expects to continue. The rest of the week was background on robotics and CPU design, useful for understanding but not actionable on its own.

## What was published

### 1. [Jensen Huang Resigns. Who Should Replace Him to Keep Nvidia Successful?](https://taekim.substack.com/p/jensen-huang-resigns-who-should-replace) — Tae Kim, 12 Nov

- **The argument**: Nvidia's board announced Huang's resignation as CEO and director, effective immediately, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO. Kim, who wrote in his book that Nvidia would be lost without Huang, says his view has changed: the engineering bench does the work and the company can thrive under the right successor. He names two: Arm CEO Rene Haas (a former Nvidia executive) or Ian Buck (the inventor of CUDA, who runs hyperscale). He says retaining Jonah Alben, head of GPU engineering, is critical. He puts the stock at about $160, approaching 10 times forward earnings, with revenue growth guided at 70% next year, and calls it "extremely undervalued even under a bad scenario".
- **The risk he flags**: the scandal may bring tighter regulation, including closing the route by which Chinese customers rent Nvidia chips in overseas data centers.
- **Checked**: the resignation and interim appointment are confirmed by [Nvidia's press release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition). Since Kim wrote, [Bloomberg, citing the FT](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/huang-discusses-comeback-as-nvidia-chief-with-allies-ft-says), reports Huang is discussing a return with allies and that the board has approached Rene Haas. I could not confirm the live share price or the 10 times multiple; secondary coverage says the shares are more than 30% below their record.
- **So what**: a governance shock at a business whose demand is intact is the kind of event a multi-year holder should look at. See the trade idea below.

### 2. [Why AI Data Center Constraints Make Efficiency More Valuable](https://www.viksnewsletter.com/p/why-ai-data-center-constraints-make) — Vik's Newsletter, 10 Nov

- **The argument**: opposition to new data centers has grown since the midterms, especially in Texas. Whether the industry is limited by power or by site approvals varies by region (Santa Clara approved 13 sites that cannot be powered until 2028 to 2030; a South Carolina project had power contracted but stalled on a county moratorium). Either way, the incentive is to get more useful compute from each approved, powered site.
- **Explainer, 800 VDC**: racks today distribute power at 48 to 54 volts. Power is voltage times current, so as racks go from about 200 kW towards 600 kW to 1 MW, low voltage means enormous current, thick copper and heat losses. Distributing at 800 volts cuts the current by more than ten times. Vik sees three stages: power "sidecar" racks next to compute racks (near term), row-level power centers (medium term), and solid-state transformers that convert grid-level voltage straight to 800 V (longer term; Envision has the first commercial deployment in China).
- **Also**: inference-specific chips raise tokens per watt. OpenAI reports its Jalapeño accelerator at 1.5 to 1.9 times Nvidia's throughput per watt (company-reported). Packing more GPUs per rack has limits: Nvidia's 144-GPU Kyber rack is reportedly delayed by its copper midplane.
- **So what**: the beneficiaries are power semiconductors and power conversion equipment, then liquid cooling and optical links between racks. Vik names no stocks in this post; his earlier paid post maps the power semi names.

### 3. [Clouded Judgement 11.13.26: The Frontier Blinks](https://cloudedjudgement.substack.com/p/clouded-judgement-111326-the-frontier) — Jamin Ball, 13 Nov

- **The argument**: frontier model pricing had held at $10 / $50 per million input / output tokens. On Monday Anthropic launched its new models at $8 / $40, a 20% cut. The models are about 30% faster, which lowers cost per token about 23% (the chip costs the same per hour and produces 1.3 times the tokens). So the cut roughly equals the efficiency gain. Ball expects labs to keep passing gains through, as Snowflake and Databricks did, because this is a land grab.
- **Software valuations this week**: median multiple 4.7 times next-twelve-month revenue; high-growth names 13.4 times; median growth 13%; median free cash flow margin 21%; median net retention 109%.
- **Disclosure**: Ball works at Altimeter, which invests in companies he covers.
- **So what**: falling token prices are good for companies that buy tokens (software applications) and for volume through the infrastructure, and they cap the labs' margins. This links to Gaetano's figure below on Anthropic's compute returns.

### 4. [The AI Selloff, Anthropic's Growth and Where I'm Looking Next](https://gaetanoresearch.substack.com/p/the-ai-selloff-anthropics-growth) — Gaetano, 9 Nov (preview only)

He wants higher lows before adding to semis and is looking for names outside AI. On Anthropic: the WSJ reports an annualised revenue run rate above $80 billion; Q3 revenue was $17 billion; revenue per dollar of compute fell to $1.72 from $2.08 because about $5 billion went on expensive short-term capacity; adjusted operating margin fell to 15% from 19%.

### 5. [CoreWeave Finally Helped the AI Trade](https://gaetanoresearch.substack.com/p/coreweave-finally-helped-the-ai-trade) — Gaetano, 12 Nov (preview only)

- **The argument**: CoreWeave has been the focus of worries that the buildout cannot be financed. Its results showed lenders still financing customer contracts, customers prepaying, paid-off hardware being re-rented, the bottom of the capex range raised, and a path to grow from backlog with 2027 capex near this year's level.
- **Checked**: Q3 revenue was $3.66 billion, up 168%, and backlog rose to about $132.5 billion from $104 billion ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-arrests-stock-slide-after-posting-a-strong-earnings-beat-and-raising-its-full-year-guidance/)). One analysis notes about $9 billion of prepayments against $14.5 billion of quarterly capex, so the funding gap has narrowed, not closed.
- **Paywalled**: his read-through to suppliers.

### 6. [Twist Bioscience Is Powering AI Drug Discovery. Is the Stock Worth the Price?](https://gaetanoresearch.substack.com/p/twist-bioscience-is-powering-ai-drug) — Gaetano, 12 Nov (preview only)

- **The argument**: Twist (TWST) makes synthetic DNA for research labs. Revenue grew 28%, AI drug-discovery revenue grew 175% for the year, and it had its first quarter of positive adjusted EBITDA. He says the stock is about 50% below its level a month ago.
- **Checked**: the [company's 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001581280/000158128026000053/twst-2026930xex991.htm) shows Q4 revenue of $126.6 million, full-year revenue of $459.4 million, gross margin of 52.4% and fiscal 2027 guidance of $550 to $560 million. I could not verify the size of the share price fall.
- **Paywalled**: his valuation work and entry level.

### 7. [An Investor's Handbook V: Physical AI](https://nuttycld.substack.com/p/an-investors-handbook-v-physical) — Nutty, 13 Nov (preview only, but a long preview)

- **The argument**: robots are being deployed first where the task is defined and measurable (Zalando returns, GXO totes), not as general helpers. The planning model can run in the cloud, but anything involving contact or balance must run on the robot, because what matters is what can change while the model is thinking.
- **Explainer, VLA**: a vision-language-action model takes camera images, an instruction and joint positions, and outputs movements instead of text. Language-model companies bring understanding and reasoning; robotics companies hold the scarce data, which is recordings of real movements and failures.
- **Economics point**: remote human assistance (as 1X uses) is a labour cost that scales with units sold unless interventions per task fall. That is the number to watch in any humanoid business.
- **Paywalled**: where the value sits (body or software) and which listed companies are priced for it.

### 8. [Why Robot Hands Are So Hard to Get Right](https://gaetanoresearch.substack.com/p/why-robot-hands-are-so-hard-to-get) — Gaetano, 11 Nov (preview only)

- **The argument**: hands are the bottleneck for humanoids. The design choice is between tendons with motors in the forearm (light fingers, but tendons stretch and wear) and motors in the hand (heavy, hot, cramped). A prototype MinebeaMitsumi hand uses 85 bearings and 11 actuators. He names ALGM, NVEC and VPG as component suppliers with named integrations.
- **Paywalled**: the sensor section and the company detail.

### 9. [When Will Next Generation DRAM Make Economic Sense?](https://www.damnang.com/p/when-will-next-generation-dram-make) — Damnang, 11 Nov (preview only)

- **The argument**: an expert call with a memory equipment engineer. The takeaways shown: the name of a DRAM structure (4F², 3D DRAM) says little about who is ahead, because cell size, yield, cost and scale decide the economics; 3D DRAM needs equipment fast enough to build the stack cheaply; HBM customers pay for performance, which justifies process costs commodity DRAM could not carry; in a shortage, equipment that removes process steps is worth more because it adds output in a fixed fab.
- **Paywalled**: all 40 questions and answers.
- **So what**: the direction favours equipment makers whose tools raise throughput, but nothing here is specific enough to act on.

### 10 and 11. [AMD's Zen 6 Family](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/amds-zen-6-family-feeding-the-beast) and [interview with Mike Clark](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/talking-zen-6-with-mike-clark-at) — Chips and Cheese, 10 and 11 Nov

- **The argument**: Zen 6 is AMD's next server CPU core. Measured single-thread performance of the EPYC 9686F is about 21% (integer) and 18% (floating point) above the previous generation's 9575F. The dense "c" core is no longer just a smaller copy: it has larger internal queues and the full cache, and the 32-core-per-chip EPYC 9996 matches the previous flagship while running 900 MHz slower, and edges Nvidia's Vera CPU. AMD doubled the units for VNNI, an instruction set used for AI inference on CPUs.
- **Explainer**: IPC (instructions per clock) is how much work a core does per tick. AMD now offers one core tuned for clock speed and one tuned for work per tick with many more cores per chip, which suits cloud and agent workloads where core count matters.
- **So what**: evidence that AMD's server CPU lead is being extended, which matters for its data center revenue. It is technical confirmation, not new information on price.

### 12. [Is This a Major Beneficiary of 24/5 Trading?](https://gaetanoresearch.substack.com/p/is-this-a-major-beneficiary-of-245) — Gaetano, 14 Nov (preview only)

- **The argument**: Nasdaq targets 6 December for extended hours. Overnight trading averaged $7.4 billion a day in August, 0.8% of US equity dollar volume. Robinhood reports almost 200,000 accounts trading through AI agents. He sees one company "in the middle of those orders" benefiting.

## Across the posts

- **Demand is not the question; financing, permitting and governance are.** Anthropic buying $5 billion of short-term capacity (Gaetano), CoreWeave's backlog up 27% in a quarter, and Kim's "overwhelming demand" all point the same way. This week's damage came from Texas permitting and the Nvidia scandal.
- **Efficiency is the common thread.** Vik argues constraints raise the value of compute per megawatt; Ball shows a 30% speed gain being passed to customers as a price cut; Damnang's expert says process-step savings are worth most in a shortage.
- **A tension to watch**: if labs keep passing efficiency gains to customers (Ball) while paying up for scarce capacity (Gaetano's Anthropic figures), lab margins stay under pressure and the value accrues to capacity owners and their suppliers. That holds only while capacity is scarce.
- **Physical AI is getting more coverage**: Nutty and Gaetano both wrote on robotics this week, and both focus on the components and economics, not the humanoid makers.

## Trade ideas

### NVDA: watch, with a view to buying in stages

- **Thesis**: the business is unchanged by the CEO's departure in the near term. Product roadmaps for the next two generations are set, demand exceeds supply, and the engineering leaders Kim names are still there. If Kim's figures are right, the price reflects a serious impairment that has not happened.
- **Source**: Tae Kim's post. The valuation claim ($160, about 10 times forward earnings, 70% growth guided) is his and I could not verify it. The staging and triggers are my inference.
- **What is priced in**: shares are reported more than 30% below their record. Check the live quote and consensus earnings before acting.
- **Why not simply buy now**: three things resolve within weeks. Earnings on Tuesday 17 November are the first look at guidance under interim leadership. The successor decision, including whether Huang returns, is open and contested. Any export restriction response from Washington has not yet appeared.
- **Suggested triggers**: a first tranche after earnings if guidance holds; more on a credible permanent CEO (Kim's criteria: Haas or Buck) and on confirmation that Jonah Alben is staying.
- **Risks**: tighter China-related export rules; a drawn-out board fight; senior engineers leaving; customers using the moment to push custom chips (see OpenAI's accelerator in Vik's post).
- **What would prove it wrong**: guidance cut on Tuesday for reasons other than China; Alben or Buck leaving; a successor from outside the industry.
- **Conviction**: medium. The setup is attractive, but the facts are four days old and still changing.
