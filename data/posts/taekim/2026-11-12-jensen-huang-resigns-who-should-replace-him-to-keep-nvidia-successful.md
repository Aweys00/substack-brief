# Jensen Huang Resigns. Who Should Replace Him to Keep Nvidia Successful?

Jensen Huang is stepping down as CEO.

On Thursday, Nvidia’s board of directors [announced](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition) that Jensen has resigned as chief executive officer and board member, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO until a permanent successor is selected. The board said its search will consider both internal and external candidates.

Jensen’s departure comes after he admitted on Sunday to being “unfaithful” to his wife, following reports that he had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. On Wednesday, The New York Times [reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair.html) that he had another affair with a subordinate, Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice president of health care. According to the Times, Jensen and Powell were seen being affectionate in public at a hotel during the GTC conference in March.

The recklessness of these two revelations proved untenable for Nvidia’s board, which moved quickly to oust him. There were likely concerns that more relationships would surface.

What happens now? The board already has a short list. After what happened with Jensen, it will likely vet candidates thoroughly to make sure they have no skeletons in their past.

In recent days, many people have cited my book as saying Nvidia would be lost without Jensen. My views have evolved in the years since I wrote those passages.

I don’t believe Nvidia’s story is over. The company has a deep bench of talent that does the work and I now believe it can continue to thrive under a new CEO. But it has to be the right leader. In my view, there are only two choices.

The first is Arm CEO Rene Haas. He has proven very successful outside Nvidia with a strong grasp of technology, a public-facing CEO skill set, and well-regarded business management experience. As a former Nvidia executive, he is also well known to Nvidia’s senior leadership, and I have heard that former Nvidia employees loved working for him. Rene would be a great choice.

The second is Ian Buck, Nvidia’s vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing. As the inventor of CUDA, Ian is one of the most important figures in Nvidia’s history. He oversees the company’s AI hardware and software products deployed with hyperscalers and other customers. He has the engineering knowledge, experience, close data center customer relationships, and employee respect needed to lead Nvidia into its next chapter.

It is also critical to do whatever it takes to retain Jonah Alben, Nvidia’s senior vice president of GPU engineering, who is essential to maintaining the company’s AI GPU roadmap. With Ian and Jonah, Nvidia’s long-term technology roadmap will stay intact.

As I write this, Nvidia shares are falling sharply, approaching 10 times forward earnings. Given the drama and history, the fear is understandable. But if the board chooses Rene or Ian as its next CEO, I have little doubt Nvidia will be positioned to keep thriving.

Some other thoughts:

There is overwhelming demand for AI compute and that demand will not disappear because Jensen is no longer CEO. This media and political firestorm may bring more regulation and restrictions on Nvidia, including closing the international data center loophole for remote Chinese customers. Even in a worst-case scenario where growth estimates take a big haircut, revenue growth will likely remain robust next year (current guidance is 70%). At the current price of $160, the stock remains extremely undervalued even under a bad scenario.

I hear Nvidia employees are shocked, disappointed and angry. For Jensen to have affairs with a Chinese citizen and with a subordinate in such public fashion is an incredible display of hubris and carelessness.

It is sad to see the tenure of one of the greatest CEOs of all time end this way. For an executive so disciplined in his business life, what a stunning downfall.
