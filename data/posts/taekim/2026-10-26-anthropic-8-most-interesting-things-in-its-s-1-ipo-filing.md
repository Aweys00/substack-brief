# Anthropic: 8 Most Interesting Things in Its S-1 IPO Filing

Anthropic’s IPO filing is here.

On Monday, the frontier AI model maker [filed its S-1 prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) with the SEC. While the 318 page document is mainly backward-looking, I believe it’s a good sign that it includes a placeholder for October ARR on page 194 (blank for now). That figure will be filled in with an amended S-1 later.

After all the talk about no more ARR updates, the ONLY reason to include October ARR in the amended S-1 is if it will help the IPO. Anthropic would be foolish to include a bad October ARR number when it doesn’t have to and it’s not foolish.

Remember that on Sept. 22, Anthropic released Opus 5.5, a much better model, which I expect is sparking a rebound in the company’s business. And when Anthropic releases Fable 5.5 soon, ARR should improve heading into the IPO.

I believe investor speculation and a bit of tea leaf reading about the October ARR placeholder helped boost AI-related chip stocks during Monday’s trading session.

Here are the 8 most interesting highlights from the filing:

-

The ticker is ANT

“We have applied to list our Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol “ANT.”

-

Anthropic touts its culture and retention numbers

“As co-founders and leaders, we aim for radical internal honesty, and we have a culture of lively and open debate between people of all levels of seniority. Every two weeks Dario, our CEO, typically gets up in front of the company and openly describes the decisions that are on his mind and the state of the industry; other co-founders and leaders frequently do the same in their respective areas. Employees debate the tradeoffs necessitated by the complexity of our mission in discussion forums that are open to the whole company. This open culture is one reason our talent density is high and our talent retention remains among the best in the industry. All seven of us still work at the company, which makes Anthropic the only major AI company to have retained its entire founding team.”

-

Enterprise adoption is on an incredible exponential revenue growth ramp

“The speed and depth of our enterprise customer adoption are powerful evidence of Claude’s position at the frontier of AI. Our customer base as of September 30, 2026 includes:

•More than 11,900 customers with $100,000 or more of revenue on a TTM basis; •More than 1,900 customers with $1 million or more of revenue on a TTM basis; and
•Approximately 250 customers with $10 million or more of revenue on a TTM basis.”

-

These self-reinforcing improvement loops are their competitive advantage

“Improvement Loop. Enterprise deployments of Claude deepen our understanding of the hard problems, edge cases, and domain-specific challenges that matter most to our customers. We improve our models, platform, and applications based on feedback from customers using our offerings in production across their businesses. These improvements result in wider deployments and adoption that fuel the next cycle of improvement.

Investment Loop. Revenue from expanding enterprise adoption helps us to fund the compute, research, and talent needed to improve Claude.”

-

Memory chip supply and TSMC chip wafer allocation are big risks

“The specialized chips required for AI workloads, particularly high-performance AI accelerators and memory, are produced by a limited number of manufacturers. The production of advanced chips is extraordinarily complex, requiring manufacturing processes at the leading edge of technological capability. The majority of the world’s most advanced chip manufacturing capacity is concentrated in Taiwan, primarily at Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (“TSMC”). This geographic concentration creates significant risks, including exposure to natural disasters such as earthquakes and typhoons, potential disruption from geopolitical tensions between Taiwan and China”

”In addition, due to the demand for advanced chip manufacturing, TSMC has, at times, been unable to fully satisfy market demand for chip manufacturing capacity, and as a result, we have not always been able to obtain sufficient quantities of chips on a timely basis.”

”Accordingly, any restrictions or delays on the supply of advanced chips out of Taiwan or TSMC would adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations. In addition, both high bandwidth memory, which is a key component in high performance AI accelerators, and other high performance memory products used in compute infrastructure, are largely manufactured by SK hynix Inc., Samsung Electronics Co., and Micron Technology, Inc. Each of these manufacturers is working to expand its production capacity to meet demand. However, there is no guarantee that these efforts will result in adequate supply fast enough to meet demand, particularly if large customers or competitors secure exclusive supply commitments or otherwise reserve substantially all of a manufacturer’s available production capacity.”

-

Inference costs come down significantly every year

“Revenue per dollar of compute has expanded meaningfully, even as we have accelerated research and product velocity and expanded our internal Claude usage. For example, our revenue grew 1,368% year over year for the three months ended June 30, 2026, compared to our compute and infrastructure expenses that grew 300% during the same period. While serving our growing customer demand, we also made significant research advances to the frontier with new leading models, including Mythos 5, Fable 5, and Opus 5, and launched new products, including Claude Cowork and Claude Design.”
