# How to Make More Money in a Volatile Market

Here’s a piece on being more tactical in a market where misleading *cough* FT *cough* FUD and manufactured narratives keep driving volatility.

One of my biggest pet peeves is hearing that the easy money has been made. It’s almost always said by people who missed the big rally in a secular growth theme. This year, that’s the AI trade.

Yes, the chip index is up 69% this year. Dell is up 356%. AMD and Intel are up 190%. Micron is up 263%, and Sandisk is up 578%. But capturing those gains has been anything but easy. It meant staying long AI chip stocks through the media’s incessant daily “AI bubble” coverage and the white-knuckle volatility of late March and late July, often sitting through multiple 30%+ drawdowns.

There is NO easy money. Every investor who stayed long deserves full credit. Anyone who says otherwise can go jump in a lake.

For most people, the best approach is to avoid overtrading and simply stay long. But for those with a bit more discipline, there’s a way to take advantage of Mr. Market’s tendency to swing between panic and euphoria.

Some quick reminders on the basics.

1. Get a good entry point. Don’t chase a stock that’s already up 30% after you decided not to buy it. FOMO is human nature, but too often I see people chase a stock after a big move, then panic puke the stock on the pullback at a big loss.

2. Diversify across three to five product-cycle winner ideas instead of going all in on one name. You get more shots on goal and less emotional attachment to any single stock.

3. Avoid leverage. It exacerbates the urge to do the wrong thing at the worst time.

4. Use sentiment to time entries. Look for overbought euphoria and oversold capitulation.

Reading the sentiment

Here’s a couple of examples. During the financial crisis, someone went on CNBC and called shorting the market at that level a risk-free way to make money. It was a strange comment after the market had already fallen so far. That turned out to be the exact low. I also noticed near almost every good low after a big pullback, I get a flurry of hate emails or snarky comments online.

The flip side holds too. After big rallies, sell-side analysts who were bearish on a stock suddenly upgrade and turn super bullish after a 30-40% run in a few weeks, even though the fundamentals have barely changed. That’s not a good sign.
