# Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa Is Not Bernie Sanders. She Supports Data Center Construction. Buy the Dip.

Meltdown. Rout. Wipeout. Those were the words in the headlines as investors lost their nerve on Thursday.

Chip stocks had their worst day in six years as Wall Street’s worries about the sustainability of the AI infrastructure buildout grew following the midterm elections.

While a Democratic sweep of the House and Senate was widely expected, the biggest surprise was Gina Hinojosa’s win over Greg Abbott in the Texas governor’s race. Uncertainty over her future policy toward data centers played a major role in the selloff as Texas is the No. 1 state for data center construction with roughly 30% of the country’s development pipeline.

Sentiment was already fragile after a month long selloff. Rather than weigh the issue in a sober, nuanced way, investors sold first and asked questions later. By the end of the session, Wall Street was pricing in Hinojosa grinding the Texas AI data center buildout to a halt.

But is the panic warranted? After watching numerous interviews and hearing her speeches, I can emphatically say no. The premise is false.

Hinojosa is not Bernie Sanders. She is a pro-business Democrat and what she says and does shows clear signs she will support business activity in the state. Her platform centers on nonpartisan kitchen-table issues. She says Democrats were elected to deliver results for working-class people on cost of living and a stronger economy for everyone, not because the party is popular.

“Because we spent so much time responding to culture war issues, people did not know what else we stood for. That’s why this campaign has been about economic opportunity for Texans. This campaign is back to basics. We are not doing the culture wars. We’re talking about how we’re going to deliver for Texans. How we’re going to make life easier for working families .. Focus on the economic issues and not take the bait on culture wars.” - Hinojosa said during a [Politico interview](https://x.com/Carloslopezjone/status/2118379750741746055)

Then, in a late Thursday interview, Hinojosa said she does not hate data centers. When asked by CNN’s Jake Tapper about the day’s stock market decline and AI investors’ fears about the economic consequences of her Texas data center policy:

Gina: “We have always been a pro-business state in Texas, but what changes now with me as governor is, it has to be a partnership that benefits Texans. And right now, what has happened is data centers have moved into mostly rural communities, and there are no rules and laws to protect Texans, and Texans are being taken advantage of. So the difference is, we will have laws to protect Texans, and we need to stop what we are doing with data centers until we have those protections. We can have a strong economy, strong industry, and protections for the people of Texas. Those things are not mutually exclusive, and I’ll make sure as governor, I lead to ensure that that happens.”

Jake: “So you’re not ruling out that data centers will be able to be built in Texas? It’s just, you want there to be regulations to protect communities, is that right?”

Gina: “And that communities have a seat at the table to decide if and how these data centers come into their communities. Texas is like a country. We are so diverse in all the ways. Different communities have different needs. And those communities have to be able to walk away from the table to leverage what they need for their community. So absolutely, we make sure they are partners in this effort, and that has always been my position. It is not that I hate data centers. I hate the way they are taking advantage of Texas and that stops when I’m governor.”

But most telling, Hinojosa gave a clear signal [during her first press conference as governor-elect today](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vj-asDC9XoQ) by introducing Ryan Pollock, a union electrician and director of the Texas State Building & Construction Trades Council, as a member of her transition team. Pollock was one of only four people allowed to speak at the press conference.

“This demonstrates my commitment to putting money in the pocket of Texans, making sure we have opportunities for good jobs,” she said of naming Pollock to the team.

This is significant. Pollock strongly supports data center construction as long as projects come with fair wages and community protections, in line with Hinojosa’s platform.

“The Texas Building Trades Council represents the men and women who will build these facilities, and we want this work” [referencing data centers, he wrote in May of 2026.](https://www.texasbuildingtrades.org/news?nID=1428&zone=news&utm_source) “That anxiety [over AI] is legitimate and the Building Trades share it, but blocking a warehouse full of computers in Texas towns doesn’t slow AI down. It just sends the construction work, the tax revenue, and the public’s leverage to demand accountable practices somewhere else, usually somewhere with fewer protections for workers and communities.”

“The hard truth is this that when a community successfully blocks a project, the data center doesn’t disappear. The buyers for that capacity still exist and the project moves to a community with less leverage and gets rolled. The same organizing strength that can shut a project down can also be used to demand strong terms like local hiring, infrastructure commitments, cost-causer pays protections, real community benefits. That’s a more strategic use of the leverage, and it produces a better outcome for the people who live nearby, the workers who build the project, and Texas as a whole.”

By naming Pollock, a big advocate of data center construction in Texas, Hinojosa was clear and explicit that she will back new projects once community protections are passed.

Then, there are the incentives. Charlie Munger is famous for saying: “Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome.”

Politicians newly elected to powerful seats generally want to win re-election. They would rather go down in history as people of accomplishment than lose the next election and become nobodies again. The best way for Hinojosa to win a second term isn’t to shut down the Texas data center economy out of performative zealotry. It’s to secure big public wins including guaranteed union jobs, no subsidies, financial concessions for local communities, and protections on water and electricity supply and costs.

This is directly from Hinojosa’s website. All the demands seem reasonable and should be acceptable to data center companies.

I expect Hinojosa will be a pragmatist. I predict the data center buildout will continue unimpeded beyond limited delays of a few months to hammer out new deals that meet the governor-elect’s demands.

OpenAI is already reading the room. On Thursday, the AI model maker [committed to being more accountable to local communities](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/community-compact.pdf) and seemed to address some of Hinojosa’s requests point by point.

Meta also hinted that some politicians will negotiate. [Dina Powell McCormick of Meta told WSJ Editor-in-Chief Emma Tucker](https://finance.yahoo.com/video/meta-president-data-center-politics-021700204.html) that a newly elected governor who ran on a data center platform said “as soon as we get past the inauguration, let’s meet, because we don’t want to lose this investment.”

I expect all the major technology companies and neoclouds to fall in line. It’s time they get to work on win-win solutions with the government of Texas and local towns and give citizens significant financial and community benefits, just as Meta and OpenAI have already done in other states. Let’s take a deep breath. This is a solvable issue for AI companies.
