# Clouded Judgement 11.13.26 - The Frontier Blinks

Every week I’ll provide updates on the latest trends in cloud software companies. Follow along to stay up to date!

The Frontier Blinks

Back in September I [tweeted](https://x.com/jaminball/status/2095599287963210198) that the GPT 6 Astra pricing launched at $10 / $50 per million input / output tokens (which happened to be the same as the other leading frontier model pricing, Fable 5.1). Despite all of the pricing wars between the big labs, and pressure from cheaper open weight alternatives, it felt like “frontier” pricing was holding more steady. However, this only lasted 2 months! On Monday Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 at $8 / $40 (a 20% cut to the then current frontier model pricing). Up until that point we’d seen steady price cuts to the lower end of the model spectrum, but the frontier has more pricing stability. So what’s changing? More competitive pressure? Something else? The new models are also ~30% faster (meaning if the same chip produces 30% more tokens per hour, the cost of each token falls about ~23%). this is the math - A chip costs the same to run per hour no matter how many tokens it produces, so if it makes 1.3x (30% more) as many tokens in that hour, each token carries 1/1.3 (about 77%) of the cost it used to, which is a 23% drop. Here, price fell 20%, so just about at the same rate. So this may just be the model provider passing through their efficiency gains. Of course - they didn’t have to! They could have just kept the frontier pricing, and generated better margins. BUT - we are in a land grab, so not totally surprising to see them pass through savings (if that is what’s going on).

I think this is something that will continue. We’ve seen versions of this from database businesses like Databricks and Snowflake. They historically called out 3 different types of efficiency gains they passed on to customers in the form of price cuts. Storage compression, better underlying chips (newer Graviton instances), and faster query times. All 3 of this drove down the cost to serve / operate, and Databricks / Snowflake historically passed those efficiency gains off to customers. I think we’ll see the same thing from the model providers. They’ll drive efficiencies, and pass through savings to ucstomesr in the form of price cuts.

Update on Multiples

SaaS businesses are generally valued on a multiple of their revenue - in most cases the projected revenue for the next 12 months. Revenue multiples are a shorthand valuation framework. Given most software companies are not profitable, or not generating meaningful FCF, it’s the only metric to compare the entire industry against. Even a DCF is riddled with long term assumptions. The promise of SaaS is that growth in the early years leads to profits in the mature years. Multiples shown below are calculated by taking the Enterprise Value (market cap + debt - cash) / NTM revenue.

Comps Output

Rule of 40 shows rev growth + FCF margin (both LTM and NTM for growth + margins). FCF calculated as Cash Flow from Operations - Capital Expenditures

GM Adjusted Payback is calculated as: (Previous Q S&M) / (Net New ARR in Q x Gross Margin) x 12. It shows the number of months it takes for a SaaS business to pay back its fully burdened CAC on a gross profit basis. Most public companies don’t report net new ARR, so I’m taking an implied ARR metric (quarterly subscription revenue x 4). Net new ARR is simply the ARR of the current quarter, minus the ARR of the previous quarter. Companies that do not disclose subscription rev have been left out of the analysis and are listed as NA.
