# The Data Center Backlash Is Here. What Happens Next?

If you own AI infrastructure stocks, these developments are something worth tracking.

A lot of the growth we’re expecting obviously depends on data centers getting built and connected to power.

If approvals take longer, projects cost more or companies have to find another location, that can change when the revenue shows up.

But how much of the buildout is actually at risk?

In [my article after the Texas election](https://gaetanoresearch.substack.com/p/hinojosa-wins-texas-what-does-this), I covered the initial implications.

Since then, I’ve been going through the interviews, local decisions and company calls to get a better sense of what could change.

There’s still a lot being built of course.

Some developers are already addressing the concerns.

Others could face a much harder approval process.

I think we need to understand those differences so we can sift through the different downstream impacts.

That means looking at what communities are asking for, what companies are agreeing to, and how far these projects have actually progressed.

Let’s get into it.

### TLDR + Table Of Contents

-

Local tradeoffs. Tax revenue can be enormous. That doesn’t resolve the complaints of people living beside a facility.

-

Who pays? Utilities need protection if developers use less power than promised. The agreements determine who carries that risk.

-

Community deals. Developers are offering water infrastructure and bill credits. Some benefits are funded, others still need approval.

-

New rules. Tax conditions, temporary pauses and possible prohibitions have different consequences for construction.

-

IREN. Real delivery progress and customer funding, alongside timing changes and contractual obligations.

-

The buildout. A substantial pipeline is underway. Earlier spending is one possible response to restrictions, but delays are another.
