# Nokia Q3 Debrief

Around 12% of Nokia’s revenue produced almost 78% of its growth this quarter.

AI & Cloud sales increased by €352M to €631M. Across Nokia as a whole, revenue increased by €454M.

A relatively small part of the business is doing most of the work on growth. Management now expects combined Optical and IP Networks revenue to grow 25–30% in 2027, with more manufacturing capacity coming to support it.

After reading the call, I feel stronger about the long term growth path. The nearer-term results were more mixed, especially cash flow. Let me unpack that. My main takeaway is that this wasn’t enough to get solid immediate stock movement. But it is encouraging for the direction of the long term investment.

When Jefferies asked about newer products for optical connections inside data centers, specifically Nokia’s LPO pluggables and external lasers, management said the 2027 growth assumption uses the existing portfolio and orders. So the 25–30% growth outlook doesn’t depend on those newer products contributing. I see them as potential upside beyond the business already supporting next year’s outlook.

AI & Cloud orders were €1.5B versus €2.8B in Q2. Management repeated that ordering is lumpy. Most orders are for delivery within 12 months, with the longer portion largely within 2027. This might be a reason why the market would punish NOK on this print.

The analyst expectations were all over the place. Inderes saw €1.5B as a positive surprise. BofA had expected more than €2B. Inderes also saw the 2027 outlook pushing forecasts higher, outweighing the weaker cash outlook in its assessment. I personally would put more weight on the delivery outlook than expecting another unusually large Q2 order number every quarter.

And some AI spending reaches Nokia through telecom customers connecting data centers. The 12% customer figure does not capture all that exposure.

### Equipment

800G coherent pluggable shipments increased 2.4x QoQ, with design wins at 29 customers. IP Networks grew 43% and added two hyperscaler design wins.

Management described each IP design win as having its own product revenue stream and life cycle. Another role inside an existing customer can become a meaningful business.

San Jose Fab 2 has completed 90% of process development. Round-the-clock operation is expected by the end of October, production by year-end, followed by volume and yield milestones in 2027.

For next year, San Jose deserves a lot of the attention. Getting more usable chips out of that investment determines how much of the demand Nokia can supply.

### Profit

Network Infrastructure revenue rose from €1.85B to €2.16B, while comparable operating profit increased from €143M to €216M.

Sales up 17% as reported. Profit up 51%.

Gross margin fell from 42.7% to 41.2%, partly because of new AI & Cloud products ramping in IP Networks. But running costs grew much more slowly than revenue, allowing operating margin to improve from 7.7% to 10%.

That is encouraging. More business spread across a relatively stable expense base helped earnings grow much faster than sales, even with lower gross margin.

Management expects the IP ramp’s margin pressure to be temporary and gross margin to improve in Q4. If sales keep growing too, that gives earnings another source of improvement. My earlier work looked toward roughly 15% Network Infrastructure operating margin in 2028. Q3 made progress toward that assumption.

The group beat had a different source. Inderes showed Network Infrastructure profit almost matching consensus, €216M versus €215M. Mobile Infrastructure produced the larger surprise, €420M versus €285M, helped by radio deliveries moving forward from Q4.

### The cash cost of preparing for growth

Nokia plans an additional €400M of working capital this year to secure supply for 2027, alongside higher restructuring cash outflows. Free-cash-flow conversion is now expected at 35–55% of comparable operating profit, down from 55–75%.

I am lowering near-term cash expectations. Money is going out ahead of the deliveries it supports. Q3 free cash flow was negative €99M, with net cash still around €2.6B. Some capital-spending payments also move into 2027, so the lower 2026 capex guide shifts part of the bill rather than removing it.

Management also described broad, structural component-cost inflation. I can understand paying earlier to secure supply, but Nokia has to manage purchasing costs and customer pricing as well as getting equipment delivered.

The €200M Infinera synergy target is expected by year-end, one year early. That helps support margins as Nokia expands the business and absorbs those cost pressures.
